Research notes

Questions I'm exploring

How can intelligent systems remain reliable beyond the lab?

My engineering work has raised questions about learning, perception, and dependable decisions on real hardware. These are directions I hope to explore in graduate study, not a list of research results I have already produced.

A

Continual and online learning

How can a model keep learning on a small device without forgetting what it knew, or learning the wrong thing?

Practical experience

  • Pill sorting: a retraining workflow for adding new pill classes.

Open problems in the literature

  • Learning new data without catastrophic forgetting means balancing stability against plasticity, and doing it with limited memory and compute.[1]
  • Test-time adaptation updates a model on unlabeled data as conditions shift; keeping it stable over long, continually changing deployments is still an active question.[2]
  • Learning on the device from IoT data streams has to cope with forgetting and data inefficiency on constrained hardware.[3]

What I want to study

Continual learning under memory and compute limits; reliable model updates on edge hardware; online adaptation in changing environments.

B

Reliable and safe physical AI

When should a machine act on its own, and when should it stop, reject or ask for help?

Practical experience

  • Pill sorting: low-confidence predictions go to manual handling instead of being sorted.
  • Lifeguard USV: possible distress postures flagged from pose estimates during survey missions.
  • Automotive and energy: on-vehicle verification, static analysis, and threshold alarms with protective logic.

Open problems in the literature

  • Out-of-distribution detection, anomaly detection and open-set recognition are closely related problems that developed separately; unified frameworks are recent.[4]
  • Conformal prediction gives statistical guarantees for learning-enabled systems. Its standard guarantees assume exchangeable data, so shifts after deployment need extra care.[5]
  • Calibrated uncertainty can tell a robot when to ask a person for help instead of acting.[6]

What I want to study

Uncertainty-aware decisions; robust perception; failure detection, safety and recovery in autonomous machines; real-time decision systems.

C

Spatial AI, AR and XR

How can a device understand the space around it quickly and accurately enough to interact with it?

Practical experience

  • Skimmer: AprilTag-guided docking on an NVIDIA Jetson Nano.
  • Lifeguard USV: LiDAR, ultrasonic and RealSense data combined through ROS2.
  • No AR or XR product experience yet. This theme is an interest.

Open problems in the literature

  • Neural scene representations such as NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting are reshaping SLAM, with open questions about real-time cost and robustness.[7]
  • Running models directly on XR headsets is motivated by connectivity, privacy and latency; recent work benchmarks the speed, memory and battery trade-offs on real devices.[8]

What I want to study

Real-time spatial perception on embedded hardware; low-latency perception and response for AR, VR and MR; accurate interaction between virtual content and physical space.

D

Sim-to-real robotics

Why does behavior that works in simulation fail on real hardware, and how can the gap be closed?

Practical experience

  • Lifeguard USV: manual-control and survey behavior tested in ROS2/Gazebo before field trials.
  • Skimmer: unattended cleaning, docking and charging cycles in field tests with K-water engineers.

Open problems in the literature

  • Sim-to-real methods can be organized by what differs between simulation and reality: observations, actions, dynamics and rewards.[9]
  • Foundation models are being explored to make transfer more reliable through more general representations.[9][10]

What I want to study

Transfer of learned behavior to real hardware; changing sensor conditions; hardware-aware adaptation; reliability in uncontrolled environments.

E

Efficient embedded AI

How much intelligence fits on a microcontroller, and what does it cost in accuracy, latency and power?

Practical experience

  • Lifeguard USV: object detection deployed on an NVIDIA Jetson Nano. Working exposure to TensorRT and ONNX.

Open problems in the literature

  • Microcontrollers often have only a few hundred kilobytes of SRAM: tight for inference and much tighter for training on the device.[11]
  • Quantization for microcontrollers spans many methods, hardware targets and frameworks, and the right trade-off depends on the platform.[12]

What I want to study

Low-power, real-time inference; quantization and model optimization; deployment on heterogeneous compute.
